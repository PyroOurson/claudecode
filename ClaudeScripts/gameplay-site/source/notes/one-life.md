# One Life: study notes

Genre model: the best-known text life simulator for phones (one life lived a year at a time).

## Session loop
- A new life starts at birth with random parents, a home city and four visible stats: Happiness, Health, Smarts, Looks (0 to 100 %). Some values stay hidden (kindness, fame, fitness, skills).
- One big button ages the character by one year. Each year writes a few lines in a journal and often shows one event card with two to four choices. The card has to be answered before the next year.
- Between years the player opens four menus: Occupation (school or job), Assets (money, homes, cars), Relationships (family, friends, partner, children, pets) and Activities (gym, library, doctor, holidays, lessons and so on).
- A life lasts 60 to 100 years in five to fifteen minutes of play. Pacing: childhood is quick (few choices), school years bring grades and clubs, 18 brings the big decision (study or work), adult years bring career, love, children and money, old age brings health trouble, retirement and grandchildren.
- Death ends the life with a summary screen and one life award (the "ribbon" in the original), then the player continues as one of their children with an inheritance, or starts a new life. The family line is kept as a family tree.

## Systems and numbers worth copying in spirit
- Stats change a little every year by themselves (health falls with age, looks peak in the twenties) and a lot from choices.
- School: yearly grades from Smarts and effort; a final exam decides university; majors lead to specific careers; medicine and law need extra years of study.
- Jobs: listings with requirements, an interview, yearly performance, raises and promotions up a ladder of titles, getting fired when performance is very low, retirement with a pension.
- Money: salary minus taxes and living costs; homes with a mortgage, cars that lose value.
- Relationships: each person has a bond bar that slowly falls unless you spend time together; actions such as spend time, talk, compliment, gift, ask for money; dating, engagement, marriage, children with names you choose.
- Health: random illnesses by age, doctor visits, chronic conditions in old age; the chance of dying rises steeply after 70 and depends on Health.

## Interface conventions
- Top: avatar, name, age, bank balance. Middle: the journal, newest year at the bottom. Bottom: the four stat bars and a menu bar with the large central Age button.
- Event cards are modal with large choice buttons; results are written into the journal.
- Phone first: everything reachable with a thumb.

## Beginner mistakes and common strategies
- Neglecting Health (no gym, no doctor) shortens the life; ignoring relationships lets bonds decay to nothing.
- Players chase a goal per life (rich, famous, big family, long life); awards reward those goals.
- Studying hard at school opens the well-paid careers; skipping university limits options but saves money.

## Our version (decisions)
- School audience: no crime, prison, drugs, alcohol, gambling or adult content. Dating starts at 16 and stays wholesome. Deaths are calm and mostly from old age.
- Original text: every event, name list and award is written for this game, in English and French.
- Hidden values: kindness (karma), fame, fitness and seven skills (music, art, coding, acting, languages, cooking, writing) unlock events, talent careers and awards.
- Each activity and each interaction with a person can be done once per year, so choices matter and spamming does nothing.
