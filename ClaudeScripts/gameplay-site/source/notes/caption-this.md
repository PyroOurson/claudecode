# Caption This: study notes

Reference: the browser meme party game Make It Meme (makeitmeme.com), its "Same Meme" mode, and the card game What Do You Meme. Nothing from them is copied: every picture is drawn in code, every bot caption is written for this game.

## Loop and pacing
- Lobby with a code, 3 to 10 players here (the original allows many more). The host picks rounds, writing time and voting time.
- A round: the picture appears (2 to 3 s), everyone writes a caption on it (60 s is the usual default, 45 and 90 are common), the round ends early when everyone is done.
- Voting: the memes are shown big, one at a time, anonymous; each player rates every meme but their own on a 1 to 5 scale, about 6 to 10 s each, faster when everyone has rated.
- Round results: memes ranked, authors revealed, points added, scoreboard with gains. 5 rounds make a game of 8 to 12 minutes.
- End: podium, then a gallery of the best memes, which players can save as pictures.

## Interface
- Writing: the picture large, the caption drawn live on it in the classic meme style (white bold capitals with a black outline, top and bottom), text boxes under it, a timer bar, the avatars of players who are done.
- Voting: one meme, five rating faces from "nope" to "legendary", a counter of votes in, your own meme shows "Your meme" and no buttons.
- Results: meme of the round with a crown, the ranking with average stars.

## Feel
- Sounds: a whoosh when a picture arrives, soft ticks in the last 5 s, a pop on submit and on each vote, a fanfare for the meme of the round, cheers on the podium.
- The fun is reading the memes together; the screen keeps the meme as large as the device allows.

## Safety (school audience)
- The original filters captions in a family-friendly mode. Here typed text is always filtered on the sender and again on the host: swear words and slurs (also with digits for letters) are masked, links, e-mail addresses, @handles and phone numbers are removed, 70 characters per box.
- The party leader can hide any caption for everyone; a hidden caption scores nothing.
- Pictures uploaded by the host are resized and size-checked on the host's device, travel only over the party connection in small pieces, are kept in memory only and never saved; every player can blur an uploaded picture on their own screen.

## Common strategies and beginner mistakes
- Short captions read best; a caption that uses the picture's details beats a generic joke.
- Beginners write long sentences that shrink to tiny text, or leave the bottom box empty when the joke needs a set-up and a punchline.
- Rating everything 5 does not help you: your own meme is rated by the others.
